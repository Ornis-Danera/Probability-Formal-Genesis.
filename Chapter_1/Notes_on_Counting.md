Notes on Counting
I have been reading and trying out quite a handful of exercises in the first chapter. And this understanding has been quite profound to me.

Firstly, I believe the chapter really tries to teach effective counting. If you can count correctly in probability problems, you have understood the foundations of probabaility problems.

Secondly, as regarding counting, I see that solving probability problems from the foundational view point of trying to actually count the outcomes following the initial permutation theorems and not jumping to the combinations formulars in some cases is the best way to grasp the essential foundamental understanding.

I also noted that we could categorize many problems into the following; it is either there is sampling with replacement or sampling without replacement. And it is either the order of sampling matters or it doesn't. And in cases where the sampling order doesn't matter, the solution most times will have permuations bleed into combinations counting.

Finally for today, I also noted that understanding the word problems in probability is very essential to solving problems correctly. For example, a question asking, "what is the probability that exactly one is chosen", is different from the question, "what is the probability that at least one is chosen". 

Also, there are cases where the problem has in its solutions, the multiplication of elements of choices and in some, just the addition. 

If one is able to see all these in problems, one has set one's self to properly understanding the foundations of probability - effective counting.
